## The Broader Phenomenon

These mechanics sit at the intersection of hyper-casual game design and mobile advertising strategy. Key patterns across the research:

- **Fake-vertising** is documented: a 2020 study found 8 of 10 games running pull-the-pin ads were actually RPGs or match-3 titles. Misleading playable ads account for an estimated 30%+ of all playable ad impressions.
- **Fail ads outperform success ads** by ~55% CPI reduction — showing the wrong move made is more motivating than showing the correct one.
- **Most honest mechanics:** Water Sort, Brain Test, Bridge Race, Jelly Shift, and Save the Girl all deliver approximately what their ads promise.
- **Worst offenders:** Homescapes/Gardenscapes (Playrix), Hero Wars (Nexters), and Evony systematically use unrelated mechanics in ads. Playrix was banned by the UK ASA in 2020.

## Cross-Cutting Psychological Mechanisms

1. **Curiosity Gap** (Loewenstein): Show a puzzle; withhold resolution. The open loop demands closure via download.
2. **Reactance** (Brehm, 1966): Show the wrong move. The viewer's correction impulse drives download intent.
3. **Zeigarnik Effect**: Near-complete puzzles are more motivating than unsolved ones. Ads show 90%-complete states.
4. **Fail Ad Psychology**: Failure activates competitive motivation more reliably than success; "I could do better" > "look how good this is."
5. **Instant Legibility**: Every successful mechanic communicates its rules in under three seconds without text.
